CASE STUDY / AI SYSTEMS · DATA QUALITY

when the agent cannot trust what it fetched.

An anonymized investigation into a systemic web-fetching defect affecting multiple AI agents, and the product decisions required to stop a tooling failure from becoming a merchant-facing finding.

web researchagent evaluationPythonreliability

THE PROBLEM

a broken fetch can look like a broken website.

multiple agents depended on shared web-fetching behaviour. when pages returned incomplete content, redirects, challenge pages or unexpected responses, downstream agents could interpret the absence of evidence as evidence of absence.

that created a product risk: a merchant could receive a confident recommendation to fix something that was actually a failure in the agent's own inspection layer.

INVESTIGATION

trace the finding back to the first unreliable assumption.

01

reproduce the defect

compare the agent's conclusion with direct browser and source behaviour instead of accepting the generated explanation.

02

separate failure classes

distinguish genuine missing content from inaccessible pages, redirects, challenge responses and parser failures.

03

look for shared causes

when the same symptom appeared across agents, investigate the common fetch layer rather than patching each agent independently.

04

validate the fix twice

the corrected behaviour was checked across multiple agent workflows and a second anonymized site context to expose hardcoded assumptions.

WHAT I CHANGED

from a vague fetch check to reproducible crawl validation.

i helped move the workflow toward sitemap-driven crawl validation with explicit response checks, retries and page-level evidence. the validation logic accounted for false signals such as shared navigation, empty shells and challenge responses instead of treating a single failed fetch as a content defect.

the important product decision was to make inconclusive a valid outcome. if the system could not establish the evidence required for a claim, the finding should be withheld or escalated rather than filled with a plausible guess.

WHY IT MATTERS

the reliability problem was systemic.

the defect was valuable precisely because it exposed a broader pattern: AI reliability depends on the quality and semantics of the tools surrounding the model. a better prompt could not compensate for a fetch layer that misrepresented reality.

the resulting playbook became reusable across related agent workflows, with regression checks designed to catch the same failure class before it resurfaced.

CONFIDENTIALITY

the technical failure is public. the underlying environment is not.
