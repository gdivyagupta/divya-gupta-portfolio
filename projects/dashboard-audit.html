CASE STUDY / AI PRODUCT · DISCOVERY

turning merchant feedback into product decisions.

An anonymized product-discovery and dashboard audit focused on understanding what users could see, what they could act on, and where the product was failing to connect AI-generated insight to useful decisions.

product discoverymerchant researchprototypinginformation architectureJira

FEEDBACK EVIDENCE

57

merchant-feedback tickets translated into product evidence.

rather than treating feedback as isolated requests, i analyzed recurring themes across a set of Jira tickets and connected them to the underlying product workflow.

The ticket count is presented as a generalized product-workflow metric; merchant and company identifiers are intentionally omitted.

DISCOVERY

from complaints to systemic problems.

i audited the product end to end and synthesized merchant and internal perspectives into 3 user personas and 4 systemic product problems: product understanding, feature discoverability, product-user fit, and whether metrics were understandable and actionable.

this changed the question from “which feature should we add?” to “which part of the workflow is preventing users from understanding, trusting or acting on the product?”

WORKFLOW MODEL

an insight only matters if the user can do something with it.

01

detected

surface what the system has found, with enough context to understand why it matters.

02

reviewed

give users a place to inspect evidence and distinguish an AI finding from a confirmed issue.

03

confirmed

make the user's decision explicit rather than treating AI output as automatically accepted.

04

implemented → verified

connect action to follow-through so the workflow can show what changed and whether the result was actually achieved.

PROTOTYPE

turning the audit into something the team could react to.

i translated the findings into a prototype using Figma and Shopify Polaris, using the existing design language rather than inventing an unrelated interface. the prototype focused on clearer workflow states, information hierarchy and actionable next steps.

i presented and defended the product direction with leadership. selected features were subsequently adopted into engineering rollout, connecting the discovery work to actual product development rather than leaving it as an audit document.

PRODUCT IMPACT

visibility and sentiment became product signals, not vanity metrics.

for one anonymized merchant engagement, tracked prompt share increased by 63% over the measurement period while negative sentiment decreased by 45%. these figures are included as observed product-monitoring outcomes and should not be interpreted as solely caused by the interface or discovery work.

the broader lesson was that product metrics become useful when they are connected to a decision: what should the team investigate, what should the merchant change, and what should the product do next?

CONFIDENTIALITY

the product reasoning is visible. the client is not.

Anonymized case study. Company, merchant, brand, dashboard screenshots, repository details and internal implementation information have been removed or generalized. Metrics are retained only where they can be presented without identifying the underlying account.
